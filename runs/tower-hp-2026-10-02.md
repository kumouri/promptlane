# Tower hp: towers fall before the Final Chorus — 2026-10-02

**Question.** Ceryce, 2026-10-02 17:57 CT: "Are towers immortal until 8:00? That's kinda of
stupid… That basically guarantees no game can do anything BUT go to sudden death. Is it because they
have too much health? Then tune the health down… And keep the new medium and tune hard to be above
it."

Towers are not immortal. Before 8:00 nobody deals enough to drop one. The Final Chorus
(`src/finale.ts`) then either awards a tower lead or starts a sudden death that divides every
structure's hp by 3. This job tunes the hp. Hard's retune is its own branch and write-up,
`runs/hard-above-medium-2026-10-02.md` (`feat/hard-above-medium`); both are measured in one Jev
block (§1.3).

- **Budget:** Jev hard stop **$4.00** for both write-ups together. Everything that can be done at $0
  is done first. Jev is spent only on §1.3's blocks, and that plan is committed before the first
  paid match.
- **Out of bounds:** `src/sim` (frozen), medium (byte for byte), the live arena and its backend
  (`:8790`, `:8797`), and the translator (other jobs are working in it).

## 0. Diagnosis, at $0

### 0.1 Where the hp lives

- `src/sim/entities.ts` (frozen) builds every tower at 900 hp, tier 1 (inner) and tier 2 (outer)
  alike, and each nexus at 2200.
- The Final Chorus rescales living towers and nexuses to a third at 8:00 when towers are level.
- So a change is a ruleset change applied from outside the sim. It goes where tower placement and
  range already live: the **map variant** (`src/mapVariant.ts`). A variant may now carry
  `towerHp: [inner, outer]` and `nexusHp`. `applyMapVariant` sets `hp` and `maxHp` before the first
  tick, and the log records the variant whole (`MatchLog.map`).
  - A variant without them doesn't touch the hp, so every older log replays exactly. All 44 Jev
    logs of §0.2 re-verify on this branch.
  - The Chorus's ÷3 rescales whatever hp a structure has, so it needs no change.

### 0.2 What the bots deal before 8:00 (recorded Jev logs, $0)

A tower's hp changes nothing in the sim until the tower dies. Towers don't regenerate, and the damage
a tower takes doesn't depend on its hp. So in a recorded match, **the first tower that would fall
at a lower hp H is the first one whose recorded hp lost reaches H**. Up to that moment the match is
the same one. The one exception is Jev, which reads the hp number in its description, and no house
or entrant rule asks about tower hp. After the first fall the match diverges, so only the first fall
is read.

The logs are the Jam stack on pvp-1: #88's 20, #90 §2's 16 and #90 §4's 8 (today's medium).
44 matches, 42 decided, read from their checkpoints every 5 s.

- **No tower fell before 8:00 in any of them.** By 7:59 the weakest outer tower had lost a median of
  **472** of its 900 (decided matches). An inner tower had lost at most 26 (two matches). No nexus
  had been touched.
- **The first tower before 8:00, by outer hp** (decided matches; inner hp doesn't matter here):

| outer hp | pvp-1: all 42 | #90 §4 only (today's bots, 8) | pvp-2: #91's 34 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 900 (today) | 0 % | 0 of 8 | 12 % |
| 750 | 5 % | 0 of 8 | 29 % |
| 650 | 7 % | 0 of 8 | 35 % |
| 600 | 10 % | 0 of 8 | 50 % |
| 500 | 26 % | 2 of 8 | 79 % |
| 450 | 52 % | 3 of 8 | 82 % |
| **400** | **67 %** | **4 of 8** | **88 %** |
| 350 | 69 % | 4 of 8 | 91 % |
| 300 | 76 % | 6 of 8 | 97 % |

- **Reading it:**
  - 750 and 650 change nothing worth having.
  - 500 gives a quarter.
  - 450 gets half of the pooled set. But eight of #88/#90's matches are near-replays of one easy
    match whose weakest tower lost exactly 476 (§2.4 there), and with today's bots it is 3 of 8.
  - **400 is the knee:** half or more on every set. Going lower (350, 300) adds little until the
    towers are paper.
  - At 400, the first fall would come at a median of 365–412 s and never before 315 s (5:15).
- **pvp-2 (#91, merged as `3ac9762` during this job):** its towers stand further forward, and the
  bots chip more. It already drops a tower before 8:00 in 12 % of matches at 900. At 600 it is half,
  and at 400 it is 88 %. Its logs used #90 §2's medium, not today's. These numbers are a $0
  counterfactual on pvp-2's recorded Jev matches. No pvp-2 match was played for this job.

### 0.3 The stand-in, after the first fall ($0, ranking only)

overstates pre-8:00 tower damage by about 1.8× (#88 §0.2), and its answers are noisy: two variants

### 0.4 The choice: `pvp-1-hp400`, opt-in

- **Outer towers 400, inner 600, nexus 2200 (unchanged).**
  - Outer 400 is §0.2's knee.
  - Inner 600 keeps "outer falls first, inner is tougher, the nexus toughest". After the first
    tower it gives a pushing side a second target it can reach before 8:00, without a lane going
    down in one push.
  - The nexus stays where it is: nothing in 44 Jev matches or 168 stand-in matches touched one
    before 8:00, so cutting it would change only sudden death, and nothing measured that.
- **Opt-in, like every ruleset layer before it** (`pvp-2`, `final-chorus-1`, the economy): a new
  named variant, `pvp-1-hp400`. `DEFAULT_MAP` stays `pvp-1`, and `pvp-1`'s logs and hashes don't
  move.
  - The name carries the number, because a changed number is a new name. A log records the whole
    variant, so it replays under the hp it was played with.
  - **Flipping the live arena** is one line in its config, `tournament.map: "pvp-1-hp400"` (it pins
    `"pvp-1"` today), and an arena restart. Nothing else reads the variant: the Jev schema server
    takes tower hp from the observation, not the map. Nothing in the arena was touched.
  - One thing to know first: the live arena's config sets no finale, economy, objective or recall,
    so it plays to 10:00 and the sim's tower-count tiebreak, not the Chorus. This job measured the
    Jam stack, as #88 and #90 did. On the arena, lower hp means more towers fall before the 10:00
    tiebreak; it wasn't measured there.

### 1.2 The target, and why

| line | pass | fail |
|---|---|---|
| **T1, towers fall before the Chorus** | the first tower falls before 8:00 in **at least half** of T's decided matches | under half |
| **T2, not trivially short** | median match length **≥ 6:00**, and no match ends before **5:00** | either fails |
| **T3, still decisive** | at least 7 of T's 8 decided (M: 8 of 8) | 6 or fewer |
| **T4, clean** | every match finishes and replay-verifies; 0 server errors; 0 parse errors | any of those |
| **T5, spend** | the whole job ≤ $4.00 on the server's ledger | over it |

- **Why half:** Ceryce's complaint is that sudden death is the only way a match is decided. Half
  makes play before 8:00 the usual way, and still leaves sudden death to the close matches it was
  built for. §0.2's forecast for exactly these 8 slots is 4 of 8, so T1 is a fair test, not a sure
  thing.
- **Why 6:00 and 5:00:** a tower lead still waits for the Chorus to win, so a short match can only
  be a nexus kill. A match decided before 5:00 would be decided before most bots' second item and
  the Bandstand's second opening. The median guards the typical match; the floor guards the worst.
- **Reported, not lines:**
  - when the first tower fell, and the earliest;
  - end reasons;
  - towers fallen a match, and inner towers before 8:00;
  - the counterfactual's forecast for each slot against what Jev did;
  - per-side structure damage and deaths beside block M.

- **The spend guard** (`pl_jev.mjs`):
  - Matches run in waves of up to 4, block by block. A wave starts only once the last one has
    finished, so nothing is in flight when the guard reads the ledger.
  - Before each wave it launches the first k slots, where ledger spend + k × m ≤ **$3.80**. m is the
    mean cost of this run's finished matches, or $0.15 before any has finished (#90 measured
    $0.12).
  - Once a slot is cut, nothing after it runs, so a cut takes HB first.
- **Never extended.** No match is added or replayed after any result is seen. A match that crashes
  before it finishes is retried once.
- Jev is not deterministic (PR #37), so a seed doesn't replay a baseline match. Comparisons are block
  against block, and seeds 3 and 7 were near-replays in #88/#90 (two or three distinct matches, not
  four).
